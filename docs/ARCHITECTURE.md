# architecture

this file is the current reading of the owner's intent and of the system that implements it. docs/FLOW.md is the dated decision log. docs/archive/SPEC.md is history. docs/HANDOFF.md holds the objective, the open owner decisions and the state of the evidence. precedence between sources: AGENTS.md. where this file and the code differ, the difference is listed in section 1 or in docs/HANDOFF.md, and neither side is assumed right.

naming rule: only `exitModule` and `exitToken`. never name or describe them anywhere.

## 1. goal, what we own, what is live

the engine exists to keep credits flowing into statements. statement sales do not need to profit. a statement that sells below the cost of its 80 credits is better than one that does not sell. unsold statements are fine: they stay in the Core and are redeemed through the exitModule in phase 2. the engine never stops buying because statements are unsold, there is no inventory gate. early on it should acquire as many credits as possible. every credit is worth the same (decision 55).

## 2. settings

one `Settings` struct in Core storage (a fixed slot shared with `CoreLib`, four packed words), one owner function `setSettings(Settings)` and the `settings()` view. every economic number is a setting. nothing economic is a constant or an immutable, except what the artcoins pool fixes at launch (below). the bounds stop typos and keep every caller payment and keeper reward capped (section 10). they are also tightened where a wider range had no honest use: the drop per credit between 1 and 1_000, `exitAfter` at least 1 hour and the buyback slice at most 1 ether. they do not bound the price the owner sets for credits (section 10). `setSettings` checkpoints the eth rate and the exit rate first, so no climb is credited under the wrong numbers, then validates every field, stores them at once and emits the whole struct. `setRate(uint256)` resets the current eth limit within [1e13, 1e18] wei per credit and at most `rateCap`, with its own event. the exit rate is held inside the band `xRateFloor` to `xRateCap` by `setSettings`, which is how the owner moves it. `rateStart` is the constructor input and the opening value.

## 3. the bid

rate is wei per credit. every credit has the same price, the read `ethRate()`. the Core reads no score and no controller on the eth doors. the exitToken bid is also per credit (section 9).

| rule | what it does |
|---|---|
| opening | `RATE_START`, constructor input in [1e13, 1e18] and at most `rateCap` (the constructor reverts `BadRate` otherwise), the market price of one credit on launch day in wei: `rateStart = price in wei`, default 8.9e15 for a market price of 0.0089 eth. it is the last fill rate and the remembered peak before the first fill |
| price state | the stored rate, the anchor, the remembered peak and the minute state. lazy and checkpointed (`CoreLib.climb`): compounded per minute from the checkpoint, up to the clamp. below the recovery level `peak * (10_000 - recoveryGapBps) / 10_000` the rate is `(1 + recoveryClimbPerMinBps / 10_000)` per minute, at or above it `(1 + climbPerMinBps / 10_000)`; the switch is solved exactly so the climb is continuous and monotone in time. a price state above the clamp holds its value. 2 percent a minute below the level and 0.5 percent at or above it, at launch values, with the level 10 percent under the peak. because the peak decays between fills, the read is not monotone across quiet periods: it can fall at most about 0.19 percent a minute at the launch half life, and sellers quote with a min out (`sellForEth(ids, minOut)`) |
| peak | the highest recent fill rate, held in `RateStore` and halved every `peakHalfLife` since the last fill or `setRate` (6 hours at launch). every fill sets it to `max(fill rate, peak now)`, `setRate` to the restated rate. it keeps the ceiling high for a while after a large sale, so the bid can climb back toward the pre sale price before the anchor settles to the last fill rate |
| ceiling | the price state stays at or below `ceilBps` of the anchor. the anchor is the higher of the last fill rate (`RATE_START` before the first) and the remembered peak now, grown by `idleLoosenBps` per full 10 minutes since the last fill, linear in the number of intervals. 110 percent, plus 2 percent per 10 idle minutes at launch values |
| clamp | `ethPot`, the price of one credit that the pot affords, read from the current pot. `ethRate()` is the price state lowered to the clamp, so the read does not fall as the pot grows and is at most the price state. the clamp is not stored: a checkpoint stores the price state, a fill pays the read and drops, anchors and starts its minute from the price state at the fill. a small pot lowers what is paid and leaves the price state where a fill at full price would leave it. a sale batch whose running total reaches the pot leaves a price of zero for the next credit, which reverts `ZeroAmount`, and the revert undoes the whole call, so the batch pays nothing |
| hard bounds | the ceiling and `rateCap` bound the read and the stored value: a lowered `ceilBps` or `rateCap` takes effect on the next read, the next checkpoint stores the bounded rate. `setRate` stores the rate, the last fill rate and the remembered peak, `setSettings` keeps them |
| drop | every credit bought, with the price state `p` at the fill, sets the price state to `p * (10_000 - dropPerCreditBps) / 10_000`, at least `dropFloorBps * minuteStart / 10_000` where `minuteStart` is the price state at the first fill of the same minute bucket (`timestamp / 60`). a `p` below that floor starts a new floor at `p`. `p` becomes the last fill rate, the remembered peak becomes `max(p, peak now)`, and `lastFillTime` is now. `buyListing` is one fill. the anchor state sits in `RateStore` (four words at a fixed slot) and is written by `CoreLib.drop` |
| gate | none. unsold statements never close the bid, stop the climb or slow a fill |

every pot change checkpoints first. accounting: `ethPot` (buying), `ethToBuyback`, `xPot` (exitToken bid), `xToBuyback`. invariant: pots never exceed what the core holds.

piles: per lane (eth, exit) an insertion ordered doubly linked list keyed by credit id. id 0 is the null sentinel and is refused at every door. per credit: lane, inPile, cost, acquiredAt. credits sent to the core outside the doors are not in a pile and are stuck.

## 4. fee intake

there is no hook of ours. the v2 skim hook takes its skim in eth on every swap and splits it into the bounty leg (to the pool's bounty recipient, the `FeeRouter`), the protocol leg (to the factory's protocol recipient, 362 of 10,000 of the skim, a separate business that is never the engine's or the owner's income) and a referral leg (cap 0). the hook pushes the bounty leg with the 2,300 gas stipend. the router's `receive()` is empty, so the push always succeeds and the hook never writes an escrow credit for it. the Core is not in the swap path of other traders at all.

numbers at the launch config, steady state, per 1 eth buy (`test/V2Port.t.sol`, `test_exactNumbersAtEachSplit`): 6.9 points of skim, so 0.069 eth. 0.0665022 eth (6.65022 points) reaches the router and 0.0024978 eth (0.24978 points) the protocol recipient. a flush pays the single payee 112,778 parts per million of the gross amount flushed (0.0074999851 eth, the 0.75 point the owner intends for the payee) and the engine the remainder, 0.0590022149 eth (5.9002215 points). `feeToBuybackBps` then splits that engine share. during the anti sniper window the router gets the whole extra (89.75022 points of a 1 eth buy at the start) and every flush in that window sends everything to the engine, no payee share. the first flush at or after `splitStart` (the mined launch time plus the window, no margin) still sends everything to the engine and turns the split on, so eth that arrived during the window is never shared.

how the Core books it:

* the Core pulls the fees itself: `sellForEth`, `buyListing`, `compose`, `composeExit` and `adopt` start with `CoreLib.pullFees(FEE_SOURCE)`, a call to `router.flush()` with at most 1,000,000 gas whose outcome is ignored (FLOW 10.8). the flush sends the eth into `receive` below before the entry point reads the pot, checkpoints or starts a measurement. a router that reverts, burns its gas or has no code leaves the entry point unaffected.
* from the router (`FEE_SOURCE`, an immutable) outside a measurement: checkpoint the rate, split the amount by `feeToBuybackBps` (that share to `ethToBuyback`, the rest to `ethPot`), resync the funded flag, emit `FeesAdded` with the whole amount. at the launch value 0 everything goes to the pot.
* from the router while a measurement is in flight (`buyListing` or an exit): revert `Measuring`. a flush started inside a measured call (by the seller of a listing, say) fails as a whole and the fees wait in the router for the next flush (V2R-1). the router is flushed by anyone, so this blocks nothing for long: it fails only inside someone else's measured call.
* anything else, the hook and the fee escrow included (donations, refunds from a purchase, a claim of the escrow credit of the Core): accept and book nothing. `skim()` books it later, to the pot whole. during a `buyListing` or an exit measurement the unbooked eth lowers the measured cost, which keeps pot and balance consistent. an escrow claim inside a measured call books nothing and the eth waits for `skim()` (`V2Port.t.sol` `test_claimMid*`); a flush inside a measured call reverts (`Fees.t.sol` `test_receiveMid*`, `ReviewV2Port.t.sol` `test_FIXED_*`). a claim worth the whole price of a listing makes the measured cost zero and that one `buyListing` reverts `BadCost`; the claimer is the seller of that listing and only loses the eth.
* `receive()` reverts only for the router during a measurement (the flush then fails whole and the eth waits in the router). for every other sender it never reverts and stays cheap. it has no reentrancy guard and returns early for any other sender.
* there is no fallback function and no `notify`: the v2 hook has no `streamForward` probe and no referral callback.
* `skim()` is permissionless and guarded. it moves `balance - ethPot - ethToBuyback` into `ethPot` and the same for the exitToken into `xPot`.
* the escrow credit of the Core (the refund of a partial fill of its own buyback, D58) is eth the escrow owes the Core. anyone calls `escrow.claim(core, 0)`, which pays the Core with the escrow as sender, so it is not booked as fees, and `skim()` then books it to the pot. nobody can send it elsewhere (`claimTo` is for the fee owner only, and the Core has no code path that calls it). a partial fill cannot be made on the launch pool in practice (it needs about 3e42 wei of input, over the int128 range of a pool amount), so the credit path is tested by crediting the Core through the real hook as depositor.
* `onERC721Received` accepts only Statements and Credits.

the router can be repointed (section 10, the router note) and the Core books only what the router sends while the router names it as engine. a second Core on the same coin and stack with the same router as `feeSource` is how the engine is replaced: the owner deploys it, calls `router.setEngine(newCore)`, and fees flow to the new Core from the next flush. the old Core books nothing more from the router, and `test/V2PortMigration.t.sol` covers that. a Core retired by `migrate` (section 10) keeps settling, selling out and burning what it holds, and its `receive` still books any fee that reaches it, so the router is repointed with `router.setEngine(newCore)` before the first `migrate`: the retired Core calls no `flush`, and a router that names it books every later fee there. `router.lock()` before that fixes the retired Core as the fee sink.

## 6. compose

`compose()` (eth lane) and `composeExit()` (exit lane, phase 2) ask the controller for a page, pull 80 credits and build a Statement through the live Statements contract. the returned id must equal `Statements.supply()` and be owned by the core. anyone may call. the gas counted for the caller payment includes the fee router flush at the start of the call, and the payment is 0 until the owner sets `callerFee` or `callerMaxBaseFee` (section 1). the statement cost is the sum of the cost bases of its 80 credits. the controller's `nextPage` read gets a fixed gas cap (500,000, in both lanes; ControllerV1 uses about 238,000 for a full page cold, measured). `Composed(sid, lane, format, cost, caller)` is emitted. the statement stays in the Core: the record holds cost, `composedAt` (the compose time), the slot in the held list, lane and `held`. holders of the coin and people in the project compose, and the owner composes when nobody does (FLOW decision 41).

## 7. the sale

the Core sells a statement through one door, `sellTo`, which only the controller calls. the controller prices the statement (`priceOf`) and a buyer calls `ControllerV1.buy`. there is no auction.

### sale controller

the asking price of a statement is set by the controller, the Core keeps custody, the hard floor and the booking of the money. `ControllerV1` has eight settings, each set by the live owner at once with its own event: `markupBps` (15_000, bounds 10_000 to 40_000, at most `startCapBps`), `startCapBps` (100_000, bounds the larger of `markupBps` and `kneeBps` to 200_000), `fastDecayBpsPerHour` (100, 1 to 10_000), `kneeBps` (10_000, bounds `floorBps` to `startCapBps`), `slowDecayBpsPerHour` (25, 1 to 10_000), `floorBps` (7_500, bounds 1_000 to the smaller of 10_000 and `kneeBps`) `anchorHalfLife` (7 days, 1 day to 90 days) and `anchorMinCost` (0.1 eth, 0 to 10 eth, a statement cost in wei). each setter checks its bounds against the current values of the others, so the order of changes matters: the cap rises before the knee rises above it, the knee falls before the cap falls below it, the floor falls before the knee falls below it, and the knee rises before the floor rises above it. these orders reach every valid combination (`test_decay_everyValidCombinationIsReachable`). its state is the anchor in bps of statement cost, 10_000 at launch, stored as `storedAnchorBps` (the anchor at `anchorTime`) and `anchorTime` in one storage slot with the settings. `anchorBps()` is the anchor now: `10_000 + (storedAnchorBps - 10_000) * 2^(-(now - anchorTime) / anchorHalfLife)`, continuous, rounded down, the same half life computation as `CoreLib.decay` on the solady `powWad`. owner setter `setAnchorBps` sets the anchor within 10_000 to `startCapBps` and `anchorTime` to now. `setAnchorHalfLife` stores the anchor now first, so a new half life applies from the change on. the start price is `startBps() = min(anchorBps() * markupBps / 10_000, startCapBps)`. the asking price is `cost * price / 10_000` where `price` is in bps of cost after `age = now - composedAt` seconds. with `start = startBps()`: when `start` is above `kneeBps`, `price = start - fastDecayBpsPerHour * age / 3600` while that is at or above `kneeBps`, and after the knee at `age_k = (start - kneeBps) * 3600 / fastDecayBpsPerHour` seconds, `price = kneeBps - slowDecayBpsPerHour * (age - age_k) / 3600`. when `start` is at or below `kneeBps`, `price = start - slowDecayBpsPerHour * age / 3600`. the price never falls below `floorBps`. it is held in units of 1/3600 bps, so each stage is an integer line of the second, and the slow stage fall is rounded up, which keeps the price non-increasing in the age (fuzzed in `test/Sale.t.sol`). at launch settings and the launch anchor the price is 150 percent at compose, 125 percent at hour 25, 100 percent at hour 50, 90 percent at hour 90 and 75 percent from hour 150. a start of 300 percent reaches the knee at hour 200 and the floor at hour 300. the price is computed from the anchor, the age and the current settings at every call, with no per-statement price stored, so a held statement follows the anchor up with sales and down with time, and a change of either rate, the knee or the floor moves it at once. `priceOf(sid)` reads the record from the Core (`statementInfo`) and returns the larger of that price and `cost * saleFloorBps / 10_000`, so `buy` keeps working when the owner raises the core floor above the controller `floorBps`. it reverts `NotForSale` for a statement the Core does not hold and for an exit lane statement. `buy(sid)` (payable, guarded) reverts `Paused` first while sales are paused (below), then takes the asking price and computes `saleBps`, the asking price in bps of cost rounded up (the price paid, not `msg.value`). when the statement cost is at least `anchorMinCost` and `saleBps` is above `anchorBps()` it stores `(anchorBps() + saleBps) / 2` capped at `startCapBps` with `anchorTime` now and emits `AnchorMoved(sid, saleBps, anchorBps)`. when the cost is under `anchorMinCost`, or `saleBps` is at or below `anchorBps()`, the stored anchor and `anchorTime` stay unchanged and the buy completes. then it calls `core.sellTo{value: price}(sid, msg.sender)` and refunds the excess last. `setStartCapBps` lowers the anchor to the new cap when `anchorBps()` is above it (stored with `anchorTime` now, event `AnchorBpsSet`). whatever the controller answers, the Core takes no payment below `cost * saleFloorBps / 10_000`.

**pause.** `setPaused(bool)` is an owner door (the live Core owner) with effect at once, event `PausedSet(bool)` on a change, `paused()` view, unpaused at launch. a call that repeats the current state changes nothing and emits nothing. while paused `buy` reverts `Paused()` before any other check, `priceOf` answers, and compose, the eth bid, exits, buybacks and fees run as before. the age in the price curve is `now - composedAt - paused seconds in [composedAt, now]`, so each held statement keeps the age it had at the pause and the fall continues from it at the resume. a statement composed during a pause has age zero at the resume. the anchor keeps its wall time decay, so the start price of a held statement follows the decayed anchor while the age is held; `setAnchorBps` and the other setters work in a pause. storage: each pause opens a window `(start, end, pausedBefore)` packed in one slot of an append only array (`pausedBefore` is the paused seconds of earlier windows, so the overlap with `[composedAt, now]` is two prefix lookups). the open window counts to now. `priceOf` and `buy` find the window of `composedAt` by binary search over window starts: one storage slot read per halving, 83,205 gas cold at 1,000 closed windows against the real Core (`test/Pause.t.sol`, `test_gas_priceOfWithManyWindowsOnTheRealCore`; 68,807 against the stand-in core of `test_gas_readCostWithManyWindows`), inside the 200,000 gas the lens gives a `priceOf` read. the window count grows by one per pause, each paid by an owner transaction, so no cap is set. storage layout of `ControllerV1`: slot 0 (the eight settings, `storedAnchorBps`, `anchorTime`) is unchanged, slot 1 holds `anchorMinCost`, `_busy` and `paused`, slot 2 is the window array length.

## 8. exit

exit (`exitStatement`). needs the exitModule. an eth lane statement must be held and `now >= composedAt + exitAfter` (else `TooEarly`). the Core hands the statement to the module and must end with at least `creditsOf(statement) * unitPerCredit` more exitToken, measured as a balance delta. an exit lane statement exits at once. `unitPerCredit` (non zero, at most uint128) and `exitToken` are read when a module is set, and `unitPerCredit` again on every later set, never in between, so a module cannot change what it owes between sets. eth lane: `exitToBuybackBps` of the received amount to `xToBuyback`, the rest to `xPot`. exit lane: `exitLaneToBuybackBps` (launch 0) of the received amount to `xToBuyback`, the rest to `xPot`. either way a share that is not zero re anchors the exit auction like any injection. the caller of `exitStatement` is paid from `ethPot` by the caller payment rule of section 1 (`callerFee`, `callerMaxBaseFee`, 0 at launch): `min(counted gas, 1,500,000) * min(block.basefee, callerMaxBaseFee) + callerFee`, at most `ethPot`. the gas counted is the call plus 50,000, so a gas burning module cannot push it past the bound. the eth is paid last, after all state is final.

replacing the module (`setExitModule`, owner only, effective at once, any number of times until `lockExitModule`). the exitToken never changes once set: a later module must report the same `exitToken()` or the set reverts `ExitTokenChanged`. the validity checks are those of the first set (code, forbidden targets, unit in range, opening price floor computed with the new unit). setting the same address again is allowed and is how a changed unit is taken over. a later set checkpoints the exit rate under the old unit first, then stores the module and the unit, and resyncs the funded flag. the exit auction price is coin per exitToken and does not depend on the unit, only the slice does: a later set never touches the start price or the clock, with or without something for sale, so `buybackExit` is never cheaper per exitToken right after a set and a mistaken unit leaves no poison in the price. only the first set opens the auction. a set also clears `allowedTarget` of the new module. pots, piles and held statements are untouched, `ExitModuleSet` is emitted every time, the old module stops being a forbidden target and the new one is forbidden from then on. while the exit measurement runs `receive()` books nothing. `exitStatement` is permissionless.


## 9. buybacks and the exitToken lane

eth buyback. `buyback()`, guarded. takes a slice `min(buybackSlice, ethToBuyback)`, at most once per `buybackDelay` blocks. the swap budget is the slice less the largest payment to the caller, `BUYBACK_GAS * min(block.basefee, keeperMaxBaseFee) + keeperFee`, and a slice under twice that payment reverts `SliceTooSmall()`, so the payment is at most half the slice. the `buybackSlice` bound `2 * (BUYBACK_GAS * keeperMaxBaseFee + keeperFee)` keeps a full slice above that line at every legal setting. the caller is paid the counted gas of the call at that base fee plus `keeperFee` (see section 1, behavior). the core swaps exact in through `PoolManager.unlock` and `unlockCallback` (the swap itself is `CoreLib.swapIn`) on the canonical pool key `(0, coin, POOL_FEE, TICK_SPACING, HOOK)`, takes the coin to itself and calls `burn` on the token, so total supply falls. the coin is restricted and the Core is off its allowlist, so the take passes through the transfer allowance the hook grants for the swap (FLOW decision 29) and burn always passes. it reverts `NothingBought()` when no coin came out. the callback returns what was spent and bought and requires the pool took no more than it was given. the fee scales by `spent` over the largest swap budget the `buybackSlice` setting allows (`buybackSlice` less the largest payment), at most the whole fee, and the gas refund is paid whole. the unspent budget and the unused part of the largest payment go back to `ethToBuyback`. `ethToBuyback` leaves only through a swap that completes: a pool or hook failure reverts `buyback()` and `ethToBuyback` stays as it was until a swap works. the hook's skim on this swap goes to the router (6.65022 points of the eth spent), and the next flush books the engine share in the Core, split by `feeToBuybackBps`. a partial fill refund would be credited to the Core in the escrow (section 4). there is no min out (section 14).

exitToken lane (phase 2 only). exit lane credits are bought with the exitToken bid and composed with `composeExit()`.

| item | rule |
|---|---|
| bid | `xRate` in bps of one credit's exit value (`unitPerCredit`) starts at 6000, caps at `xRateCap`, floors at `xRateFloor`, climbs `xRateClimbPerHour` while funded (its pot affords one credit at the rate) and drops `xRateDropPerCredit` per credit. a credit is paid `xRate * unitPerCredit / 1e4` |
| exitToken auction | there is no exit pool. the buyback of the exitToken is a dutch auction inside the core, paid in coin that the core burns |
| slice | `min(xToBuyback, exitSliceCredits * unitPerCredit)` |
| price | coin wei per exitToken unit, wad scaled. `price(t) = startPrice * 2^(-(t - startTime) / xAuctionHalfLife)`, continuous, never reverts, reaches zero for long gaps |
| cost | `coinIn = ceil(slice * price / 1e18)`, at most the caller's `maxCoinIn`. `buybackExit(maxCoinIn)` does `burnFrom(msg.sender, coinIn)` on the coin, then the slice goes to the caller. no tip, no delay, the caller approves the core first |
| restart after a fill | `startPrice = max(2 * clearingPrice, previousStartPrice / 4)`, `startTime = now`, at least 1 |
| clock | runs only while `xToBuyback` is not zero |
| injection | every time exitToken is added to `xToBuyback`: `startPrice = max(price now, startPrice / 4)` and `startTime = now`. injected funds never inherit a decayed clock |
| first start price | set when the first module is set: the price at which one full slice costs the whole coin supply. setting a module reverts `BadModule` if this is below 1e12 (computed with the unit of that set). a later set never touches the start price or the clock (section 8) |
| half life change | `setSettings` re anchors the curve at its price now when `xAuctionHalfLife` changes |
| views | `exitAuctionPrice()`, `exitAuctionQuote()` returning `(slice, coinIn)` |
| after `migrate` | `xToBuyback` stays in the retired Core and `buybackExit` sells it there (FLOW decision 52). a successor books every exit token it receives into its own `xPot` |

the restart rule means the next auction can never start more than 4x below the start of the previous one, so a price that decayed to dust does not carry over. a taker who fills near a fair price restarts the auction at twice what they paid, and the cadence is one slice per half life. the same quarter floor applies to every injection.

| rescue coin | `rescueCoin(to, amount)`, owner only, guarded, event `CoinRescued`. sends coin the Core holds to `to`. the Core holds coin only in passing (the buyback burns what it buys in the same call), so this reaches only coin that an allowlisted holder sent to it. `to` must be a listed address, because the Core is not. coin only, never eth, credits, statements or exitToken |
| rescue nft | `rescueNft(token, id, to)`, owner only, guarded, event `NftRescued`, sends with `transferFrom`. a credit leaves only while `creditInfo(id).inPile` is false, a statement only while `statementInfo(id).held` is false, every other ERC721 the Core holds leaves. a token whose `ownerOf(id)` does not answer with the Core is refused. reverts `InPile`, `Held`, `NotHolder` |
| successor | `setSuccessor(address)`: the contract that `migrate` sends the assets to, zero or an address with code that is not the Core, the exit module, the exit token, the fee source, the coin, Credits or Statements (`BadSuccessor`), reverts `Locked("successor")` after `lockSuccessor()`. unset at launch. `lockSuccessor()` is one way and allowed while the successor is zero, which disables `migrate`. events `SuccessorSet`, `SuccessorLocked` |
| migrate | `migrate(maxCredits)`, owner only, guarded, reverts `NoSuccessor` while the successor is zero, callable again until nothing is left. the first call sets the one way flag `retired` (view `retired()`, event `EngineRetired(successor)` once), and the Core then reverts `Retired` at `sellForEth`, `buyListing`, `sellForExitToken`, `adopt`, `compose` and `composeExit` (FLOW decision 48). a retired Core keeps `exitStatement`, the sales of statements through the controller (`sellTo`), `skim`, the fee booking of `receive`, `buyback`, `buybackExit`, the owner functions, `rescueCoin`, `rescueNft` and later `migrate` calls. moves to the successor: `ethPot` by one plain call, `xPot` by `transfer`, and up to `maxCredits` credits from the head of each pile by `transferFrom`. `ethToBuyback` stays in the Core and `buyback` burns it. `xToBuyback` stays in the Core and `buybackExit` sells it there, the exit token burn auction of the retired Core (FLOW decision 52). a `transfer` that reverts or returns false leaves `xPot` in the Core, logs `ExitTokenMigrationSkipped(successor, amount)`, and the eth leg, the credit leg and the retirement go on (`Migrated` shows 0 exit tokens); the next `migrate` retries it. `exitStatement` on a retired Core pays its caller from what is left in `ethPot`, capped at the pot and often 0 after a `migrate`. the held statements and their records stay in the Core, which sells or exits them itself (FLOW decision 33); a sale books its proceeds at once and a later `migrate` moves the pot share. the rates are checkpointed before the pot is zeroed. eth and exit token above the trackers and the coin stay. event `Migrated(successor, eth, exitTokens, credits)` with `eth` the `ethPot` moved |
| the fee router | a separate contract with its own owner (launch: the same key). `setEngine`, `setPayees`, `setSplitStart`, then one way `lock()`. see the router note below |
| sale settings | on the controller: `setMarkupBps`, `setStartCapBps`, `setFastDecayBpsPerHour`, `setKneeBps`, `setSlowDecayBpsPerHour`, `setFloorBps`, `setAnchorBps`, `setAnchorHalfLife`, owner of the Core only, at once, each with an event |

the owner is the first owner of the launch config until a handover. **the hard rule: eth, credits and exitToken leave the Core at the owner's call only through `migrate`, to the successor, and coin and NFTs only through `rescueCoin` and `rescueNft`.** no function sends them to the owner. the owner directed transfers are `rescueCoin` (coin that an allowlisted holder sent to the Core, FLOW decision 28), `rescueNft` (an NFT the Core's books do not hold, FLOW decision 32) and `migrate` (FLOW decision 33), whose destination is the successor the owner set until `lockSuccessor()`. that holds under every combination of settings, because every other outflow is capped by a bound: the `buyListing` caller payment by the bid less the listing cost, by `keeperFee` (0.005 eth at most) and by `keeperMaxBaseFee` (100 gwei at most) times the 1,500,000 gas cap, the caller payment of `compose`, `composeExit` and `exitStatement` by `callerFee` (0.005 eth at most) and by `callerMaxBaseFee` (100 gwei at most) times its gas bound (13,700,000 gas for compose and composeExit, 1,500,000 for exit), the buyback caller payment by `keeperFee` (0.005 eth at most) and by `keeperMaxBaseFee` (100 gwei at most) times the 600,000 gas cap, and credit purchases only pay sellers of real credits at the bid price, and the credits stay in the Core. the controller is read only for the Core: it names credits to compose and prices statements, and the Core enforces every limit itself. what the owner controls here is trust, not custody: a module set by the owner receives statements, and must return at least `creditsOf(statement) * unitPerCredit` of a token the owner chose.

**the successor switch.** until `lockSuccessor()`, the owner can set any contract with code as the successor and call `migrate`, which moves `ethPot`, `xPot` and the credits the Core tracks, in one transaction or in batches, with no delay. the first call retires the Core: the six doors that buy credits, adopt them or compose refuse with `Retired`, and `ethToBuyback` and `xToBuyback` stay for the Core's own `buyback` and `buybackExit`. the held statements stay in the Core and are sold out there through `sellTo` and `exitStatement`. fees that arrive later are booked, and a later `migrate` moves the pot share. `lockSuccessor()` with the successor zero, or before the old Core has sold out, leaves the pots and credits that arrive later unmovable. this is the second owner switch, with the router's `setEngine`, that directs value to an address the owner picks. it exists to move the engine to a new Core, and the router switch moves the fee stream with it. a stolen owner key can move everything the Core tracks at once. mitigations: a multisig owner, the public events `SuccessorSet` and `Migrated`, and `lockSuccessor()` when no migration is wanted any more (with the successor zero it disables migration). at launch the successor is zero and unlocked.

**what the rule does not say.** the owner sets the price the engine pays for a credit, and the owner may sell credits to the engine. so a dishonest owner, or a stolen owner key, can drain the eth pot without transferring anything out: it raises the limit (`setRate` up to `rateCap`, `rateCap` up to 1e18, `dropPerCreditBps` down to 1 with `dropFloorBps` at 10_000) and sells credits to the engine at an inflated price through a seller it controls. the credits stay in the Core but are worth a small part of what was paid. the bounds limit the price per credit, which the pot caps: the read is at most `ethPot`, and every spend is a spend of the pot, so the whole pot goes in one transaction (`test_ACCEPTED_ownerCanOverpayAnAccompliceSeller`: 10 eth pot, `setRate` to the launch `rateCap`, the whole pot paid in one block for credits worth about 1 eth). after that every eth of fee inflow can be taken the same way. holders therefore trust the owner key, the same trust as for the token admin. the owner accepted this (no raise guard on the settings, they are adjustable at once). a multisig owner and public `SettingsSet` and `RateSet` events are the mitigation.

**trust note.** with no delay the owner key controls everything at once: it can point the exitModule at a contract that returns dust and take every statement, swap the controller and sell every statement at the hard floor, lower the hard floor to its bound, and overpay for credits as documented above. the exit token has the same shape as the overpay drain: the owner sets `xAuctionHalfLife` short (10 minutes at the bound) and raises the unit with `setExitModule` (a later set leaves the start price of the exit auction as it was, and the slice grows with the unit), and an accomplice then buys `xToBuyback` with `buybackExit` for little coin. a stolen owner key means the whole engine at once. the owner chose this: the system is new and must adapt fast, and he will announce changes off chain. holders trust the owner key fully. the three locks close doors later, and a handover can move the owner to a multisig, but until then nothing stands between the key and the engine.

**the router note (FLOW 10.2, in this strength).** the router owner can point every future fee at any address with one call until the router is locked. it never touches what an engine already holds. this and the coin admin's `setBountyRecipient` on the hook (section 14 item 11) are the two owner switches in the system that direct value to an address the owner picks. `coin.lockRecipients()` seals the pool side switch right after the launch, and `router.setEngine` stays open as the migration switch for a later engine until `router.lock()`. FLOW decision 8 is amended to say so. the same owner key can also repoint the payee list (at most 200,000 parts per million of a flush after the split starts, so the engine keeps at least 80 percent) and the split start, all until `lock()`. a stolen router owner key redirects the whole fee stream at once, with no delay, and so does a stolen coin admin key until `lockRecipients()`. the Core's holdings, the pots and the statements are out of its reach. mitigations are the same as for the Core owner: a multisig, public events on every setter (`EngineSet`, `PayeesSet`, `SplitStartSet`, `Locked`), and `lock()` when no migration is wanted any more. the router needs an engine set before `lock()` (a lock can never strand the eth) and a flush against an engine that refuses eth reverts and the eth waits, swaps are never affected.

what the owner can do at once is change every economic number: the bid, the sale floor, the split of proceeds, the pace. holders trust the owner not to do so against them, the same trust as for the token admin. every change is one event with the whole struct, so it can be watched.
