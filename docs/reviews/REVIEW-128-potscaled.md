## verdict

Not fit to merge as is. Three things block it. First, `_toBpsRaw` brings back an overflow that can freeze `setSettings` and the balance bid under a module answering a huge `available()`; this is a one line saturation fix. Second, the repo's calibrated model shows the shipped rule buys about 40 percent fewer credits per eth (cost/market 0.89 to about 1.35), and that trade-off is missing from decision 128 and needs the owner's explicit sign-off. Third, both pump proofs (`test_pumpThePotAttackIsUnprofitable` and the sim's pump scenario) are independent of the multiplier, so as written neither supports the claim in decision 128.

## cost of the variants

From the repo's calibrated model `simulate()` in sim/engine.js (sellers paid at the bid, the same fee path), 90 days, the mean over seeds 1 to 3. Cost/market is the sum of what the engine paid over the flat market value of the credits bought; credits per 290 eth normalizes the count to a fixed spend; peak bid/market is the most any single purchase paid over market. The "loosening only" variant is a model-only flag (`multiplierScalesClimb` false) because scaling the loosening but not the climb is not a one-line change in the shared `CoreLib` body; the contract scales both.

| variant | cost/market | credits per 290 eth | peak bid/market |
|---|---|---|---|
| a before this branch (climb 50, loosen 200, no multiplier) | 0.84 | 35,195 | 1.76 |
| b shipped (climb 100, loosen 600, m on both, cap 4) | 1.17 | 23,541 | 4.67 |
| c m on the ceiling loosening only (climb 50, loosen 200, cap 4) | 0.84 | 35,195 | 1.76 |
| d c with loosen 600 | 0.79 | 38,416 | 1.67 |
| e m on both, cap 2 (climb 50, loosen 200) | 1.02 | 27,384 | 3.52 |
| f m on both, cap 4 (climb 50, loosen 200) | 1.14 | 24,288 | 3.96 |

The shipped rule (b) is the most expensive: about 33 percent fewer credits per eth than before the branch (23,541 against 35,195) and a peak bid of 4.67x market. Scaling the ceiling loosening alone changes nothing at the old climb and loosen (c equals a): at a 0.5 percent per minute climb the price rarely reaches the loosened ceiling, so a faster loosening does not raise the realized bids. The cost comes from the faster climb (the 100/600 base and the 4x on the climb), not from the loosening. The owner picks the point on this curve.
