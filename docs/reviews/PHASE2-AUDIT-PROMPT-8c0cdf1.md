## What is being audited

Three new contracts deployed beside an already live, unchanged Core on Ethereum mainnet, plus the scripts that deploy and wire them:

- `src/adapters/Phase2BalanceTreasury.sol`: the Core's balance module and the custodian of the BALANCE token. It converts Statements the Core hands it (through the upstream token's `withdraw`), keeps every received token in custody while earmarking `conversionToClaimBps` of each receipt as a claim reserve, pays the Core's Credit sellers and the artist from working funds (`pay`, with the inherited debt accrual for failed transfers and `claim`), and lets any holder burn CC through `redeemCC` for a share of the claim reserve capped at `floor(ccIn * claimReserve / totalSupply)`, with a replaceable quote policy consulted under a bounded static call. Owner controls: three switches, contribution bps, fee bps, minimum working balance, policy replacement, `fundClaims`, `allocateToClaims`, `returnClaimsToWorking`, `withdrawWorking`, `rescueToken`, and a `migrateTo`/`receiveMigration` handshake to a successor treasury. Authority is the Core's live owner. A transient storage reentrancy lock covers every mutation.

- `src/ControllerV2.sol`: a replacement for the live `src/ControllerV1.sol` (compare them): the issue 113 live spot and live liquidity guard in `_protected` (`P_buy = min(prior block sample, live spot, reference + tol)`, `P_sell = max(prior block sample, live spot, reference - tol)`), a persistent Statement ask `max(cost floor, ceil(reference * statementSaleMultiplierBps / 10000))` with no decay or anchor, age only redemption eligibility (`atFloor`), and derived legacy views (`markupBps`, `decayHours`, `startCapBps`, `anchorBps`, `ageEligibilityOn`).

## Properties to break

Attack each one. For every property either show a concrete path (inputs, state, sequence, the exact line) or state precisely why none exists.

1. Custody. BALANCE leaves the treasury only through: `pay` to the Core's recipient bounded by `available()` (free working funds); `claim` of recorded debt; `redeemCC` against an actual CC burn, at most the proportional share; `withdrawWorking` of free working funds; `migrateTo` to a verified successor. The claim reserve and the recorded debt are never spendable by `pay`, `withdrawWorking`, `allocateToClaims`, `rescueToken` or any policy. Unsolicited inflows only raise working funds. Consider every ordering of the Core's two `pay` calls in one `sellForBalance`, the Core reading `available()` once per batch, and a hostile recipient.
2. The Core's receipt check. `Core.redeemStatement` requires the module's physical balance to rise by at least `rating * unitPerPoint * (10000 - redeemSlippageBps) / 10000`; the treasury must pass it with its internal earmark and must refuse a short receipt. Reentry through the upstream token's `withdraw` (the Statements transfer, the artwork callback, the token's `_transfer` from itself) into any treasury function, with the Core's lock and the treasury's transient lock both in place.
3. `available()` never reverts, is zero before selection, before the token's transfer gate opens (finalized retirement, funding phase 3, a reached nonzero `transfersOpenBlock`), when migrated, retired or insolvent, and fits the Core's 2,000,000 gas bounded read. `pay` plus its debt accrual fits the Core's 600,000 gas artist bound against the real token's transfer cost (the previous pin measured about 300,000 gas for a transfer with ink splitting and a first receipt's artwork mint; the authors estimate, they did not measure). The transient lock must not block the Core's two sequential `pay` calls.
4. `redeemCC`. Every refusal (expired, stale epoch, zero or oversized input, bad recipient, switch off, not the selected module, migrated, transfers closed, zero output, policy above the cap, policy revert, short return, gas exhaustion, short or failed payment, short or failed burn, insolvency, reentry through the token callback) reverts with no state change. A successful burn reduces `totalSupply` by exactly `ccIn`. Backing per remaining token does not fall at zero fee. The fee stays in the reserve. The restricted coin's `burnFrom`: confirm its allowance and restriction semantics on the real token (the authors burned CC bought through the canonical hook route on a fork without an allowlist entry), and consider supply changes inside the same transaction (the Core's own buyback burns, hook behavior). Can any caller or policy extract more than pro rata, or grief others (make the reserve unspendable, block `claim` or `pay` for a third party, poison the epoch)?
5. Owner controls and the epoch. Every mutation is evented. `configEpoch` advances exactly on policy change, fee change, `returnClaimsToWorking`, `migrateTo` and `receiveMigration`, and a burner's pinned epoch, minimum and deadline are honored. `returnClaimsToWorking` requires CC redemption off. `setRedemptionPolicy` turns redemption off and its probe cannot be fooled in a way that matters given the treasury's own cap. `rescueToken` cannot touch CC, BALANCE, Credits or Statements. Is there any owner action that silently changes a quoted burner's terms without an epoch change? Is there any way to trap the owner's controls?
6. Migration. `migrateTo` requires every switch off on both sides, a successor bound to the same coin and token and to the same Core (or the Core's recorded successor once the Core has retired), naming this treasury as its immutable `predecessor`, not migrated; refuses unless custody minus debt covers the whole reserve; zeroes the reserve and sets `migrated` before transferring; `receiveMigration` accepts only from the recorded predecessor and only if actual custody covers reserve plus debt plus the import. Check: double imports, a successor that is not yet or no longer the Core's selected module, debt left funded, late inflows to a migrated source, and the retired Core path (`selectedForClaims` versus `selected`).
7. ControllerV2. The six Core probes (`nextPage` within 500,000 gas, `atFloor` within 400,000, `pastWindow`, `priceOf`, `valuePerPoint`, `balanceClampFor`) behave and fail closed. `CoreLib.setSettings` reads `markupBps()` to bound `saleFloorBps`; V2 answers the multiplier clamped to uint16. Attack the price rule with same block and persistent pool manipulation (real Uniswap v4 swaps on the fork, as `test/ControllerV2.t.sol` does) through `buyCredit`, `buyCredits`, `buy`, `sellForEth`, `sellForBalance` and the balance clamp, around a due reference refresh and with the live liquidity gate; the authors claim a same block move only worsens the mover's own side and a persistent move cannot leave an early Credit ask below a later bid. Check view and door parity. Check the Statement ask and eligibility rules, the multiplier floor of 10,000, a zero delay, and anything ControllerV1 offered that the Core, the keeper or the web still needs and V2 dropped.
8. Deployment and wiring. `Phase2Deploy` creates with plain CREATE; `Phase2Readback` checks pinned masked runtime hashes (`script/Checks.sol`), bindings, owner resolution, solvency, the hazard state (`balanceBidOn` true with `valueModeOn` false and a pool key set) and samples executable payouts; `Phase2Settings` patches exactly three fields. Can a wrong or malicious BALANCE token address pass the deploy preview? Can the readback pass against a treasury bound to a different Core or coin, or against altered code? Does `SetSettings.s.sol` on a selected V2 do anything it should not? Is the seven artifact launch pin set unchanged (`test/BuildIdentity.t.sol`)?
9. Off chain. The web's `redeemClaim` captures consent before any await, approves exactly `ccIn` only when the allowance is short, refuses a changed epoch, a non executable treasury or a quote under the minimum, and decodes the receipt from the treasury's own event; the keeper skips `redeemStatement` while `conversionReady()` is false and treats an old module without the view as ready; the indexer writes state from events only. Look for a path where the UI could sign something other than what it displayed, a simulation that could be bypassed, or an indexer state that could diverge from the chain.
10. Economics as implemented. The claim reserve uses the total supply (pool holdings included) as the denominator. The arbitrage path buy CC, burn, sell BALANCE is the intended holder benefit when the reserve exceeds the market price. State any way the design lets a party extract value from the reserve beyond the proportional share, or lets the owner's listed powers do something the disclosure (docs/SPEC.md section 1 phase 2 rows, docs/RUNBOOK.md "Phase 2") does not say.
