# Independent system audit: pinned main 84ea170

**Evidence frozen 2026-10-18. Verdict: not launch ready.** The full candidate suite passed, but the deep campaign ended with one invalid invariant model, the documented web defaults select the wrong hook, resume omits a pinned-owner check, and the planned phase-two settings contain an impossible spread/tolerance pair. The local lifecycle passed 37 checks under explicit impersonation, pre-funding, synthetic liquidity, manual clock changes and a subsidized keeper. Those checks establish local mechanics, not production service acceptance or market incentives. No production contract behavior was changed for this audit.

## Scope, provenance and method

The audited checkout was freshly cloned with recursive submodules from latest main at the start of testing. Its exact commit is **84ea170ab3330a4163ef8ac00cb5fa2cd067fe09** (merge commit dated 2026-10-18 19:36:49 EDT). The fresh checkout was clean before tests. The audit branch was cut from the same origin/main commit; the report and owner-instruction log are its only intended tracked edits.

I read the current SPEC and DECISIONS for intended behavior and challenged their economic and liveness assumptions. AGENTS required reading STATUS first; it contained historical review summaries. I did not open previous audit reports, extract their findings, or use them as a checklist. Findings below came from pinned source, independent source-to-sink tracing, static analysis, tests, local runtime, and independent review of consequential claims.

The production scope included Core, both linked libraries, ControllerV1, settings bounds, adapters, StatementBalanceModule, interfaces, renderer, the factory/hook integration, deployment and recovery scripts, the keeper, indexer, website and their records. The external Artcoin source was compared to live runtime. Jack's balance token was built from its pinned source for local tests. This report includes confirmed defects, conditional risks, misleading tests and claims, and gates that remain unproven. A proposed behavioral repair or naming cleanup belongs in a separate change after this frozen report.

## Independent threat model and asset routes

The owner key can immediately edit economic Settings, controller, balance module, adapters, pause, locks and successor; the same EIP-7702 delegated signer controls the deprecated factory launch. A compromised or mistaken owner can deliberately overpay a seller it controls, choose a hostile module, or retire into a successor. The absence of a timelock is an explicit trust choice with immediate asset consequences. An untrusted seller, buyer, order maker, keeper, swapper, token recipient, marketplace target, RPC/provider and web API user can influence distinct boundaries. The balance token's funder and Statements owner are separate launch authorities. A keeper cannot be assumed to subsidize work.

| Asset and source | Authority and external boundary | Sink or failure to inspect |
|---|---|---|
| ETH trading fee | Artcoin hook partitions skim; push to Core can fail into fee escrow | Core.book credits pot, redeem-burn and payee; collectEscrow must remain callable and incentivized |
| ETH pot | Public fund and fee booking; owner Settings control bid and reserve | Direct sellers, listing target, artist, keeper tips, compose/redeem caller payments, successor migration |
| ETH buyback/redeem reserve | Statement/credit sale split and fee setting | Uniswap v4 buyback and burn, owner release; pause, fee ceiling, liquidity and empty reserve can delay actual burn |
| Credits NFTs | Sellers, marketplace orders, CreditStrategy, direct swaps | Core pile, Statements composition, sales, rescue, successor; approval, return lock and offerer identity matter |
| Statements NFTs | Composition and controller sale or holder redemption | Buyer, Core-held pile/sale, upstream withdrawal in module; price floor and pause apply differently to sale and redemption |
| BALANCE token | Jack funder retirement and withdrawal; module custody | Seller and artist payouts, debt claims, pool liquidity, successor module; only eligible after retirement and opensAt |
| CC coin | Factory launch and pool swaps | Owner/admin parameters, LP positions, buyer holdings, buyback burn; miner and pool-price influence around public calls |
| Offchain decisions | Keeper listing feeds, RPCs, indexer events, website record/key | Transactions, alerts and displayed prices; stale or unbounded feeds can suppress action or mislead a buyer |

The main hostile-call surfaces are Core's bounded controller and adapter staticcalls, the marketplace target.call with ETH, Credit/Statement ownership and score reads, external token and module calls, v4 PoolManager swaps, hook fee pushes, and keeper/API responses. Core's acquisition doors use a nonReentrant latch, compare NFT ownership and balances after listing calls, and cap spending. Controller's paid sale doors use their busy latch. Those guards address common callbacks, while findings below identify boundaries where fail-open or inaccurate accounting remains.

In the pinned Artcoin source, deprecated-factory deployToken is owner-only and atomically creates the coin, pool, record and launch liquidity (ArtCoinsFactoryV2.sol:148-206). Its CREATE2 salt includes msg.sender and configHash; a mutable tokenDeployer changes predicted addresses (same file:274-276,631-643). The factory passes the 690-bps skim, 9,000-bps bounty share, Core bounty recipient and protocol recipient into the hook (same file:448-467). ArtCoinsHookV2.sol:389-421 sends the baseline bounty and any anti-sniper extra to Core; protocol receives the baseline remainder. The ETH leg uses a 2,300-gas push and credits failed delivery to fee escrow (FeeDelivery.sol:24-35), so collection is a liveness door rather than missing income. Before its coin settings lock, the coin admin can change bounty recipient/referral and lower baseline fee (ArtCoinsHookV2.sol:588-631); the signed Core LaunchConfig hash alone cannot bind all later mutable factory/coin economics. Preflight and postflight pin initial values, and a live lock/authority readback remains necessary.

The pinned Jack token's funder-only retirement, Statements ownership/format registration, and opensAt gate cross different authorities from the Core owner. The production module checks actual token balance change on redemption and seller payment, and subtracts owed liabilities from available. Its direct transfer failure can accrue artist debt; a whole failed call cannot. A swappable controller and module are owner trust boundaries, not neutral oracles. Onchain pool spot/refreshed reference can be moved by traders and LPs; the 1.1498-ETH synthetic pool and one round-trip assertion do not establish resistance to other MEV orderings or liquidity withdrawals. Public bots face room/reserve checks, return locks, 2-minute keeper listing delay, 3-to-5-gwei refund ceilings, optional zero caller fee, pause and retirement; a valid onchain call can still be uneconomic or suppressed by offchain ranking.

## Confirmed security and economic findings

### S-01 · Medium · Value-mode BALANCE clamp fails open on controller call failure

**Location:** src/lib/CoreLib.sol:581-590; src/Core.sol:1151-1157; test/ValueMode.t.sol:580-595. **Claim:** value mode is supposed to limit the flat BALANCE payout by a protected controller quote, but a failed, false or malformed bounded balanceClampFor call returns the unbounded posted payout. Core.setController probes nextPage only. The existing hostile QuoteController lacks balanceClampFor; test_hostileQuoteIsBounded installs it and then sells for BALANCE successfully. Its assertions check the ETH rate cap and unchanged posted balance rate, not the value-mode payout clamp. **Failure scenario:** a controller replacement or degraded quote path allows a sale at posted payout when the protected value cap would be smaller, transferring excess module reserves to a seller. This is conditional on owner selection or a failing external controller; the owner is already a trusted economic actor. **Structural fix:** require a valid clamp answer in value mode, probe the required controller surface at installation, and test a quote failure with an explicit lower expected cap.

### S-02 · Medium · Artist royalty can vanish if the whole bounded module call fails

**Location:** src/lib/CoreLib.sol:1153-1157; src/adapters/StatementBalanceModule.sol:113-130; test/BalanceFlat.t.sol:75-94. **Claim:** Core ignores the success flag of the 600,000-gas artist pay call. The production module records owed[to] only if its pay function completes; a whole-call revert or out-of-gas creates no debt. A 100-token seller payout at the configured 250-bps purchase royalty owes **2.5 tokens** to the artist. The existing test uses MockBalanceModule, which keeps the 2.5 in custody after a blacklisted transfer but records no owed liability; it still claims later payment or recovery. **Failure scenario:** a module/token/artist path that exceeds the bounded call or reverts before debt recording permits a completed Credit purchase without its artist obligation. A normal Jack token transfer inside the 600,000-gas budget was not shown to fail; this is a conditional boundary defect. **Structural fix:** require the module call itself to succeed; keep recoverable transfer failure inside the production module and assert debt or actual receipt in the test.

### S-03 · Medium · Resume skips a pinned owner-delegation safety gate

**Location:** script/deploy.sh:274-278; script/Checks.sol:128,137-146,387-405. **Claim:** deploy.sh tolerates full Preflight failure in RESUME modes, but preflightResume omits _preOwner. On an isolated fork, four deployment transactions landed before interruption. Replacing the owner's 0xef0100... delegation with empty code made standalone Preflight exit 1 (delegation mismatch and failed keyCount read), yet RESUME=state sent eight more transactions, passed postflight and launched the coin while owner code remained empty. The complete path used **21,031,361 gas** in both interrupted and uninterrupted runs. **Failure scenario:** a resumed launch can bypass the trust assumptions checked on the initial run after an owner delegation or key-registry drift. The local impersonation does not establish that an outsider can sign for the owner. **Structural fix:** keep current delegation, delegate-code hash and keyCount checks in resume and launch-only preflight, while treating already-deployed address rows separately from new critical owner checks.

### S-04 · Medium · Replacing a live module strands its unowed reserve from live bids

**Location:** src/Core.sol:1404-1423; src/adapters/StatementBalanceModule.sol:143-159. **Claim:** setBalanceModule changes the sole live module pointer without moving the old module's reserve. Old module pay is Core-only, and migrateReserve needs a retired Core and compatible successor. **Failure scenario:** an owner rotates the token/module while the old module holds funds; the new BALANCE bid sees only the new module's available amount, while the old reserve cannot serve the active Core. The owner may switch back before lock or later retire and migrate a compatible reserve, so this is not unconditional permanent loss. **Structural fix:** require an explicit funded migration/drain path or prohibit replacement with an unowed old reserve; verify successor token compatibility before locking.

### S-05 · Medium · Permitted BALANCE discount can make each acquired Credit loss-making

**Location:** src/lib/SettingsBounds.sol:41-43; src/lib/CoreLib.sol:1138-1157; docs/DECISIONS.md:373-379. **Claim:** balanceDiscountBps may be zero while purchase royalty is 250 bps. At par, a 100-token claim costs the module **102.5 tokens** to acquire (100 to seller, 2.5 to artist) and can redeem for at most 100, or **98** at the allowed 2% shortfall. At exact par the discount must be at least **244 bps** to cover a 2.5% royalty applied to the discounted payout; at a 2% redemption shortfall it must be at least **440 bps**. The 1,000-bps launch choice has margin, but the editable range does not. **Failure scenario:** an owner setting within bounds repeatedly converts module reserves into claims below acquisition cost. **Structural fix:** encode a royalty/slippage-aware minimum or require an explicit owner acceptance and solvency monitor for below-break-even choices; this changes economics and requires owner decision.

### S-06 · Low · A permitted listing price can leave no permissionless keeper tip

**Location:** src/Core.sol:740-761; keeper/src/decide.ts:251-290. **Claim:** buyListing caps keeper payment by bid minus value. At a 0.0049 ETH bid and equal 0.0049 ETH listing, the allowance is zero, while execution still consumes gas and the artist royalty is paid separately from the pot. **Failure scenario:** a listing that meets the onchain price condition cannot attract a rational unaffiliated bot at normal positive gas cost; the owner keeper also skips when its margin test fails. **Structural fix:** define an explicit caller payment budget independent of discount, lower the acquisition threshold to leave a gas margin, or explicitly narrow the public sweep promise.

## Offchain, integration and launch-readiness findings

### L-01 · High · Website default pool hook does not match the deployed hook

**Location:** web/lib/config.ts:50; script/config/mainnet.json:4; web/lib/v4.ts; web/scripts/v4-selfcheck.ts:17,30. **Claim:** the documented web default is 0x2f7976bf73dE8e1D49D4c687404e527a75b0e8Cc, while the pinned live hook is 0x866F31562dDe70D7B989C2529a9deFFFFd3bE8CC. The example environment leaves the override unset. The local-env generator supplies the correct record value during rehearsal, masking the default. The self-check derives the pool ID from its own hardcoded key and compares it with itself. **Failure scenario:** production web built from documented defaults quotes or routes against a different pool, disables trading, or displays incorrect spot data. A private production override could correct it but was not available to verify. **Structural fix:** require the deployed pool key from a signed deployment record at build/startup, check onchain hook and pool identity, and fail closed on mismatch.

### L-02 · Medium · Invalid top listings can starve a valid keeper candidate

**Location:** keeper/src/listings.ts:228-235. **Claim:** the keeper ranks and slices its first 40 watched listings before checking adapter support, ownership or status. In a focused 41-listing reproduction, 40 higher-ranked invalid entries consumed **120 reads** across two blocks, **40 invalid / 0 valid each block**, and the valid 41st was never read. **Failure scenario:** stale or adversarial feed entries persistently prevent the permissionless keeper from seeing a profitable order. **Structural fix:** validate before the cap or page until 40 valid candidates have been examined, with bounded total work and cache invalidation.

### L-03 · Medium · Listings API has no bound on rejected upstream pages

**Location:** web/app/api/listings/route.ts:90-105. **Claim:** the route limits accepted entries to 200 but has no page or fetch-time bound. Five mocked pages containing 500 rejected rows caused five upstream requests and zero results, contrary to the two-page comment. **Failure scenario:** invalid upstream orders or a slow provider can hold the route through platform timeout and make listing discovery unavailable. **Structural fix:** cap requests/time independently of accepted-row count, cancel slow fetches and expose partial-result status.

### L-04 · Medium · Indexed BALANCE outflow may exceed actual token transfers

**Location:** indexer/src/index.ts:140-142; src/lib/CoreLib.sol:1153-1157. **Claim:** BalanceFill.paid + toArtist is summed as actual outflow, though a bounded artist module call can accrue debt or fail while tokens stay in the module. For a 100-token seller payout and 250-bps royalty, the indexer reports 102.5 tokens even when only 100 leave; the 2.5 may be owed or absent. **Failure scenario:** dashboards and accounting based on that counter overstate settled transfers and miss unpaid royalties. **Structural fix:** separately label nominal obligations, actual Transfer deltas and module debt.

### L-05 · Medium · Failed alert delivery consumes the full throttle window

**Location:** keeper/src/alert.ts:11-18. **Claim:** a key is throttled for 1,800 seconds before the webhook POST succeeds. With a mocked HTTP 500 on the first attempt, two same-key alerts produced only **one POST**. **Failure scenario:** a transient alert endpoint failure suppresses the next operational warning for 30 minutes. **Structural fix:** mark delivery only after success and use bounded retry/backoff with a separate failure signal.

### L-06 · Low · Indexer omits module-origin BALANCE Transfer history

**Location:** indexer/ponder.config.ts:115-122. **Claim:** the raw token Transfer filter selects from=Core, whereas the production module sends seller/artist payouts, claims and reserve migration. **Failure scenario:** a transfer-history view is incomplete even though Core-event activity remains indexed. **Structural fix:** subscribe to the configured module and token, or make the event-derived ledger's narrower scope explicit.

### L-07 · High · The phase-two rehearsal does not run the approved activation tuple

**Location:** script/dev/E2EReal.s.sol:364,499; script/dev/e2e-real.sh:329-345; docs/DECISIONS.md:368-384. **Claim:** the run wires the module with feeToRedeemBurnBps still **0** rather than the chosen 1,000; keeps credit spread **1,000** and balance bid cap **10,000** rather than the intended switch values; enables value mode before two healthy reference refreshes; and records only token/module, not a complete phase-two pool transition. Its successful redemption cannot prove the intended fee-funded burn reserve or safe activation order. **Failure scenario:** operators treat a 37/37 local pass as evidence for a different, potentially reverting or unusable phase-two configuration. **Structural fix:** decide a compatible phase-two tuple, script and assert the ordered owner actions, update the deployment record and run focused fork checks on that exact tuple.

### L-08 · High · Anti-sniper fee funding in the lifecycle depends on a subsidized trader

**Location:** script/dev/E2EReal.s.sol:113-132; script/dev/e2e-real.sh:143,190-203; script/config/mainnet.json:86-96. **Claim:** an impersonated, pre-funded trader makes ten 0.5 ETH buys and five sells inside the **90%** anti-sniper window, delivering about **4.666495090607153611 ETH** of fee income to Core before booking. The keeper then has a funded bid and can sweep a 0.036 ETH listing against a 0.0963518997 ETH bid. The test does not establish that real traders will voluntarily pay that fee, that external bots will leave that discounted listing, or that funder/owner actors hold the required assets. **Failure scenario:** real launch has little fee income, an unfunded or capped bid, and no keeper work despite the passing local path. **Structural fix:** publish a launch liquidity/trade/incentive model and verify the funding threshold under realistic trading and adversarial competition; label donated fork liquidity separately from organic fee income.

### L-09 · Medium · The fork harness can issue a transaction envelope above its own mainnet gas cap

**Location:** script/dev/E2EReal.s.sol:475-493; script/dev/Scenario.s.sol:618; script/Checks.sol:48-50. **Claim:** raw creation uses a **25,000,000** gas limit while the configured mainnet transaction cap is **16,777,216**. The pinned Jack initcode estimated at **6,883,955 gas** on the restored fork, so actual construction appears below the cap, but a 25M-limit transaction cannot itself be the claimed mainnet-sized envelope. **Failure scenario:** a local pass hides envelope rejection at the intended block gas policy. **Structural fix:** set every rehearsal transaction gas limit at or below the production cap and assert both limit and gasUsed from receipts.

### L-10 · Medium · Final service state was not preserved for cross-service acceptance

**Location:** script/dev/e2e-real.sh:57-62,94-136,372-378; deployments/1.json. **Claim:** KEEP=1 printed that Anvil would remain, but the process exited with the command session. ### L-11 · Medium · Chain-id-1 indexer cannot be pinned to the local fork from its record

**Location:** indexer/ponder.config.ts:29,68-101. **Claim:** IS_LOCAL is true only for chain ID 31337. A chain-id-1 fork therefore uses public mainnet endpoints first; PONDER_RPC_URL_1 is only the last reserve, and record.rpcUrl is ignored. An audit-only Ponder process using the matching pre-phase record made **213 requests to Tenderly, 41 to Blast, five each to 1rpc/PublicNode/dRPC, and zero to local Anvil**. Its /ready and /health/parity returned **503**, and it aimed at block **26,223,038** while the restored local fork was **26,208,392**. **Failure scenario:** a rehearsal silently mixes live mainnet events with a fork deployment record, yielding false parity or never becoming ready. This is a test/configuration boundary, not evidence that the production indexer is broken on mainnet. **Structural fix:** add an explicit fork-only RPC mode that accepts chain ID 1 but pins every read to the record's local RPC and fork head; refuse public fallbacks in that mode.

## Test validity, comments and names

### T-01 · High verification · Deep ceiling invariant checks the wrong clock

**Location:** test/invariant/Invariants.t.sol:513-520,632-643; src/lib/CoreLib.sol:715-725. The deep campaign's single failure followed owner pause and a roughly 76-second warp. Production _climb freezes its growth clock at pausedSince; _ethCap in the invariant continues decaying the peak and counting idle time from wall block.timestamp. It compared an actual frozen ethPrice of **29,164,450,608,786 wei** to a model ceiling of **29,161,110,459,659 wei**, a **3,340,149,127 wei** difference (0.01145%). Independent source review found this is a model error, not evidence of a runtime breach of the pause rule. **Failure scenario:** a release gate fails or a developer weakens production code to satisfy an invalid model. **Structural fix:** compute the model at pausedSince while paused and include the climb-restart anchor; add a deterministic pause/warp regression and rerun only the affected invariant. The original 54/1 deep result remains reported, not rewritten as a pass.

### T-02 · Medium verification · Gas-cap test does not assert its claimed batch size

**Location:** test/GasCap.t.sol:177-236. The test name/comment promises about 91 Credits fit under the transaction cap, but _searchBatch only finds a boundary and asserts that n+1 exceeds the cap; it never asserts n >= 91. It could pass at a much smaller safe batch. **Failure scenario:** operators plan a 91-credit rescue/migration batch from a green assertion that did not establish that throughput. **Structural fix:** assert a concrete minimum batch at the selected profile and track its measured gas.

### T-03 · Medium verification · Balance-token replacement tests compare wrong or equal-by-default values

**Location:** test/BalanceTokenReplacement.t.sol:182-193,221-224. One test claims the shared instance resets on token replacement but enters replacement already at its floor. ## Local lifecycle: what was and was not real

The chain ID, factory, hook, pool manager, Credits, Statements, CreditStrategy, Seaport execution, v4 swaps and fee booking were real forked bytecode at block 26,208,025. The CC stack and Jack token were deployed from pinned source to **local Anvil only**. The keeper's real binary selected and submitted local transactions. A signed EIP-712 Seaport order was fulfilled through the real venue. The fork performed 10 coin buys and 5 sells, booked hook fees, swept a strategy listing, filled Seaport, sold Credits directly, enforced the return lock and reserve, composed a page, sold a Statement, burned CC in a buyback, surrendered Jack's launch page, redeemed at the floor, added a full-range BALANCE pool, switched value mode, refreshed reference, sold for BALANCE and ETH, and tested a same-block round trip.

The owner, funder, Statements owner, CreditStrategy and user wallets were **impersonated** on Anvil. The script **pre-funded** them, supplied synthetic BALANCE/ETH liquidity, advanced time manually, controlled base fee at 1 gwei for keeper decisions, and manually triggered keeper ticks. The phase-seven pool moved **23,702.51121 BALANCE** plus **1.1498088187971 ETH** from a funder account whose balance read **24,950.0118 BALANCE**; the total token supply read **53,750,000 BALANCE**. The 23,702.51121 moved amount was about **0.0441% of total supply**, contradicting the script comment that says about 2% of supply. It produced a spot move from 49,000,000,000,000 to 52,986,170,305,599 wei per token and six keeper refresh ticks. A 246.5604 BALANCE seller payout and 6.16401 BALANCE royalty were asserted. The same-block round-trip check measured a **177,461,682,905,660 wei** pool loss in that constructed pool; it does not bound all transaction ordering, token distributions, liquidity withdrawals or market prices.

The lifecycle's 37 passing assertions do not show that a real funder can burn the required launch amount, a public actor supplies the pool, outside traders accept the 90% fee, a rational bot pays gas at the chosen door tips, a production keeper relay lands transactions, an indexer catches up, alerts arrive, or the website reads the final record correctly. Jack's token retirement is funder-only: the pinned source requires that actor to burn **2,386.7072 BALANCE** before ordinary holders can withdraw, and opensAt is 2026-10-20 14:20 UTC. The E2E impersonated and funded that actor, then warped past opensAt. Production custody, approvals, timing and readiness remain independent gates.
