## Provisional choices made during repair

| choice | recommendation implemented for local review | consequence to accept before launch |

Do not reuse a stale last-good rate or a live spot outside the protection rule. | A missing or failing BALANCE pool/controller quote can stop the ETH bid until pricing recovers or the owner turns value mode off. |
| Credit return lock | Apply the configured lock to a Credit sold by the engine across every intake door, even after a transfer to another holder. | A buyer cannot sell that Credit back to the engine within the launch 6-hour lock, including after a legitimate transfer. Reconfirm the market anchor on deployment day. |
| Keeper uncertain transactions | Persist sent hash and nonce, stop further paid sends until the receipt is reconciled, and fail closed on malformed spend records. | RPC/relay trouble can stop the keeper and require operator recovery. It prevents nonce reuse and budget resets after an uncertain send or corrupt ledger. |
| Keeper listing cooldown | Cool only deterministic order-specific failures. Let transport and receipt uncertainty follow transaction recovery. | A bad listing waits before retry; service outages do not silently hide otherwise valid listings for five minutes. |

| ETH bid near the reserve | Keep `bidOf` as the seller's indicative payout, and require callers to check payout plus the on-top artist royalty against pot room. The keeper already checks total listing cost and simulates before sending. | A positive `bidOf` can still be unfillable when the room covers the seller but not the royalty. This is a liveness and display edge, not an ETH solvency breach. A firm executable quote would require a separately reviewed pricing/interface change. |

## Phase-two pricing change required

The frozen-candidate stateful invariant found a Credit bought for 26,865,198,878,560,711 wei and later sold to the ETH bid for 27,853,905,102,552,017 wei, a 988,706,223,991,306-wei gross gain (3.68%). The test moved the pool spot without a real swap, from its initial quote toward 667,945,987,706,385,938 wei per BALANCE token. Later refreshes raised the reference from 514,500,000,000,000,000 to 625,377,965,625,000,000 wei per token. It set the return lock to zero to probe round trips. Therefore this is a valid counterexample to the claim that every round trip loses, but not proof of net profit after a real swap, gas, a six-hour production lock, and price persistence. The six-hour lock is not a mathematical closure: sixteen 2% refresh steps can raise the reference about 37.3% in 160 minutes at the proposed 600-second cadence.

The current spread rule protects a round trip at one executable quote. The prior-block ask may lag a persistent upward pool move while later bids catch up. The owner chose to keep this pricing for the initial launch, when value mode is off, and require a change before phase-two activation (decision 197). A live-spot upward guard for Credit sale pricing or refusal is a proposed design, not an approved implementation. Select and test the phase-two rule with real swaps, actual pool liquidity, return lock and gas before value mode is switched on. The costless test move is not evidence of profitable real-world manipulation.

The live-spot rule can be implemented in `ControllerV1` without changing Core or CoreLib. Since the controller is not a proxy, a post-launch repair would deploy a new controller and call `Core.setController` while `controllerLocked` is false. The replacement's sale settings, anchor, pool key, samples and reference start fresh; review their initialization and switch timing with held inventory. Keep `lockController()` unused at phase-one launch if phase two will use this Core. Once locked, the Core cannot adopt a revised controller.

A focused Foundry test used the real v4 pool manager and swap router on the pinned fork. Its test account supplied the pool liquidity and was pre-funded with ETH for the upward swap; the Credit buyer did not pay the swap cost. With the phase-two 203/100-bps pair and a six-hour return lock, the moved spot was 637,000,000,591,243,917 wei per BALANCE token; a Credit bought for 11,728,779,715,408,140 wei resold after 36 ten-minute reference refreshes for 14,799,414,643,736,273 wei, a 3,070,634,928,328,133-wei gross gain (26.18%). This demonstrates a persistent-market repricing path, not a net attacker return or production pool-depth result. The test sits in `test/ValueMode.t.sol` and the full affected file passes 49/49 on the local fork.

PR #111's prelaunch reset is incorporated selectively; its removal of resume owner checks is not adopted. PR #112's fail-closed quote and cross-holder return-lock ideas are incorporated selectively; its conflicting decision numbers and unrelated changes are not adopted as a whole.

At the 2026-10-19 read-only GitHub check, both proposals remained open: PR #111 head `4e75de0b4c0d31179d11e533883c20099d17941d`, PR #112 head `02f31d1aa72502cc0018309a9ea52ce6362b3bbf`. The candidate branch contains separately committed selective repairs and is not a merge of either PR.

The owner already chose the phase-two 203/100-bps spread/tolerance, same-token inactive BALANCE reserve recovery on request, rollback when the artist royalty module call wholly fails, and the royalty/slippage-aware BALANCE break-even relationship with wide individual ranges. The relationship makes a 10,000-bps redemption shortfall incompatible with the maximum 9,999-bps discount while balance acquisition is enabled; no test should assert that impossible combined settings tuple is valid.
