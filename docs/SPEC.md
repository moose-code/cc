# spec: the credits engine as built

the current reading of the owner's intent and of the system that implements it. docs/DECISIONS.md is the dated decision log and the authority on intent; this file cites its numbers. docs/STATUS.md holds the plan, the freeze table and the state of the branch. precedence between sources: AGENTS.md. the vocabulary is "balance", never "exit".

## 1. the product

CC is a coin whose trading fees fund a permanent public bid for Credits. anyone sells Credits to the engine at that bid, and the engine sweeps cheaper listings on approved venues, keeping the discount. the engine composes 80 Credits into a Statement, holds it, and sells it at a price that starts above cost and falls to a floor the owner sets. a quarter of every sale burns CC and three quarters buys more Credits. when the balance token is live, Statements at their floor redeem into it by rating, the engine buys Credits with it by score at a flat posted price, par minus `balanceDiscountBps` (1,000 at launch, decision 164), and every redemption burns CC from a reserve. the owner sets every number at once, with no delay; holders trust the owner key, and one way locks and a successor migration are the owner's ways to narrow or move that power. the engine launches before the balance token; the balance bid and chosen composition ship built and switched off; the credit sale window and the redeem switch are on at launch, and redemptions revert `NoBalanceModule` until the module is set. the balance token is held by its module, not the Core. (decisions 99 to 117, DECISIONS.md section 11.)

## 4. the bid

the rate is wei per 100 points (`RATE_SCORE` = 1,000,000 score units). `ethRate()` is the read: the price state lowered to the pot, where the pot is `ethPot` plus the pot share of unbooked fee income. a credit of score `s` is paid `ethRate() * s / RATE_SCORE` (`bidForScore(s)`); `ethPerPoint()` is `ethRate() / 100`; `bidOf(id)` is `bidForScore(scoreOf(id))`. the eth doors read the score from the CreditScore contract (`scoreOf(seedOf, timestampOf)`, the same source the balance side reads), 800,000 to 8,000,000, never 0. the doors book first, then read the bid of the booked pot.

| rule | as built |
|---|---|
| opening | `rateStart`, the market price on launch day of 100 points in wei (4.9e15 at the pin), in [1, uint64.max] and at most `rateCap` |
| per credit | a credit of score `s` is paid `ethRate() * s / RATE_SCORE` (`bidForScore(s)`, `bidOf(id)`); `buyListing` caps the quote at it; `adopt` records `ethPrice() * s / RATE_SCORE`, at least 1 wei |
| price state | the stored rate climbed per minute since its checkpoint: `recoveryClimbPerMinBps` below the recovery level, `climbPerMinBps` at or above it, compounded, continuous at the crossing, up to `min(ceiling, rateCap, pot)`; a price state above that bound holds its value. the climb is not scaled by the pot multiplier |
| pot multiplier | `m = clamp(pages, 1, climbMultiplierCapBps / 10,000)`, the pages the pot covers at the last fill rate, where one page is `pointsPerPage` points costing `lastFillRate * pointsPerPage / 100`: `pages = pot / (lastFillRate * pointsPerPage / 100)`. the last fill rate is the rate credits last cleared at; it changes only on a fill, so `m` is fixed between fills and a checkpoint never moves the read (the displayed bid matches the paid bid). the pot is `ethPot` plus the pending fee share for the eth instance and the module `available()` as bps of par for the balance instance. `m` scales the ceiling's idle loosening only; the per minute climb is unscaled. `climbMultiplierCapBps` is the owner cap, 10,000 to 65,535, launch 40,000 (4x). `climbMultiplier()` returns `m` for the eth instance |
| peak | the highest recent fill rate, halved every `peakHalfLife` since the last fill or `setRate`; every fill sets it to `max(fill rate, peak now)` |
| ceiling | `ceilBps` of `max(last fill rate, peak now)` grown by `idleLoosenBps * m` per full `idleStepSeconds` interval since the last fill |
| clamp | the pot is `ethPot` plus the pot share of the unbooked fee income the next `_book` adds, less the reserve (`reserve()`, the `reserveWei` row of section 3), over `1 + buyToArtistBps / 10,000` so a bid the read shows is payable with its artist royalty on top (decision 163, one rule for the climb, the read and the price basis reads of decision 158); the price state climbs toward it and the read is at most it, so the rate climbs on fee income the hook has pushed before a `book` folds it into `ethPot` (decision 147). a sale whose running total passes the pot reverts whole |
| drop | a credit of score `s` bought at price state `p` sets it to `p * (10,000 - dropPer100PointsBps * s / RATE_SCORE) / 10,000` (the fall bounded at 10,000), at least `dropFloorBps` of the price state at the bucket's first fill. the drop tracks the eth spent |
| floor rate | `max(price state, peak now)`: the rate under every price floor of the engine (`floorRate()`) |
| reset | `setRate(rate)` restates the stored rate, the last fill rate and the peak |

the same price state machine runs as two instances, sharing every climb and drop setting above (`climbPerMinBps`, `recoveryClimbPerMinBps`, `recoveryGapBps`, `dropPer100PointsBps`, `dropFloorBps`, `ceilBps`, `idleLoosenBps`, `peakHalfLife`) and the pot multiplier `m`, each instance reading its own clamp as the multiplier's pot. the eth bid is the instance above: the unit is wei, the cap is `rateCap`, the clamp is the pot, the opening value is `rateStart`, and `setRate` resets it. the second instance is the shared instance of value mode (decision 164): its rate is bps of par, the cap is `balanceBidCapBps`, 10,000 at launch and adjustable up to uint32.max, the floor and opening value is `10,000 - balanceBidMarginBps`, and in value mode it is the eth bid's price state, read by `sharedRate()` and multiplied by the controller's value per point. the balance bid does not read it: `balanceRate()` is the posted `10,000 - balanceDiscountBps` and `balanceBidOf(id)` is `score * unitPerPoint * balanceRate() / 10,000` (section 9). a change of `balanceBidMarginBps` that raises the floor above the stored rate resets the instance to the new floor.

value mode (`valueModeOn`, decision 148, off at launch). the eth bid prices off the shared balance instance: `ethRate() = min(bps * valuePerPoint(false) * 100 / 10,000, rateCap)`, lowered to the room (`ethPot` plus the pending pot share less the reserve), where `bps` is the shared instance climbed to now with its clamp at `balanceBidCapBps` rather than the module's funds, and `valuePerPoint(false)` is the controller's wei value of one point at the protected buy price, read by staticcall with 200,000 gas. a failed, short, zero or not ok answer no longer reverts: `ethRate`, `ethPrice`, `bidOf` and `sellForEth` fall back to the last good value rate kept in the eth instance checkpoint, so a lapsed refresh degrades pricing instead of halting the engine (decision 159, C02); the controller's sale doors still revert `NoQuote`. `sellForBalance`, compose, redemption, the buyback and `book` never read the eth bid. a fill through `sellForEth` or `buyListing` drops the shared instance by the per credit drop, floored at `10,000 - balanceBidMarginBps`, and leaves the eth instance as it was (`ValueRateFill(spent, rateBps, pot)` in place of `EthRateFill`). the instance climbs in value mode whatever `balanceBidOn` and the module; turning `valueModeOn` on resets it to the floor, a settings edit or a module set leaves it. `adopt` records the value mode price, and `sellForBalance` records the value mode rate as the cost basis, the last good rate while the quote is unusable. the balance token payout is lowered to the eth bid for the same score valued in tokens at the protected sale price per token, `min(posted, bidForScore(score) * 1e18 / P_sell)` (decision 165): paying tokens is the engine selling tokens, so the payout is worth at most the eth bid when sold and a cross-currency round trip cannot extract a spot that leads the reference. the conversion and the `min` are the controller view `balanceClampFor(score, posted)`, read by a bounded staticcall; the Core keeps `posted` when the read fails or the reference is unusable, where the sale doors revert `NoQuote` and no round trip opens. `balanceBidOf` and `balanceBidForScore` report the clamped amount, so the view equals the fill. outside value mode the payout is par, unchanged.

the controller's protected price: `referencePrice` starts at the pool spot when `setBalancePoolKey` runs, provided the live spot is within 4.5 percent of the owner's expected spot independently of `tolBps` (decision 161), and `refreshReference()` (public, also run first by `buy`, `buyCredit` and `buyCredits`) moves it toward the spot by at most `min(referenceRateBpsPerHour * elapsed / 3600, referenceStepBps)` bps of the reference. buys price off `P_buy = min(sampledSpot, referencePrice * (10,000 + tolBps) / 10,000)`, sales off `P_sell = max(sampledSpot, referencePrice * (10,000 - tolBps) / 10,000)`; the executable spot is sampled at a prior block. the quote is usable while `referenceMaxStale` is nonzero, the reference is at most `referenceMaxStale` old, both recent pool liquidity samples are at least `minLiquidity`, and the spot and the module unit read. `spotPrice()` reports the spot. the eth bid pays at most `cap * score * unit * P_buy` and the sale floor asks at least `(10,000 + spreadBps) * cap * score * unit * P_sell` over the same base, with `P_buy <= P_sell`, so an eth round trip loses at least the spread. a balance round trip takes the payout in tokens, whose par value realizes at the live spot; the balance payout is clamped to the eth bid at `P_sell` (decision 165) so it too loses at least the spread, the cross-currency lead removed.


### ETH purchase budget

Both `sellForEth` overloads and every adapter listing share one ledger. At the first successful purchase in `floor(timestamp / bucketSeconds)`, snapshot the booked buying pot and set `spendBudget = floor(snapshotPot * spendCapBps / 10,000)`. Fee booking happens first; buyback and redeem reserves are excluded. Count the complete ETH outflow, including listing keeper payment. The first purchase of a bucket always passes whatever its cost; the budget is still snapshotted and the spend recorded. Each later credit raises `SpendCapReached` when spent plus cost exceeds the budget, and the entire batch or failed venue purchase rolls back. Equality succeeds. A budget below one credit's price, or rounded to zero, therefore permits one purchase per bucket. Balance-token purchases are excluded.

Same-bucket deposits and fee income cannot enlarge this snapshot. `setRate` and unrelated settings preserve it and the used amount. Changing the cap immediately reprices the original snapshot, preserving spent. At 10,000 bps enforcement is bypassed, including against later deposits, but outflow still counts if enforcement is restored. A duration edit first expires the ledger under its old duration; an active ledger is translated to the new bucket with its snapshot and used amount retained. Both bid instances' active drop-floor anchors translate likewise; expired anchors clear. An ordinary new bucket resets lazily on its first successful purchase.

`spendState()` returns `(bucket, budget, spent)` for the current bucket: the stored values while the bucket is open, otherwise zero spent and the budget `(ethPot + pending) * spendCapBps / 10,000`, where `pending` is the pot share of the unbooked fee income the next `_book` adds (decision 147). A purchase books that income first, so this is the pot the first purchase of the bucket snapshots and the budget it enforces.

`ethRate()` and `bidOf()` remain price quotes. A quoted purchase after the first of its bucket can revert because the remaining aggregate budget is insufficient. A bucket cap is not a rolling-window cap: adjacent buckets can both spend around their boundary. The counters and stored budget are checked uint128 values; theoretical outflow or budgets above uint128.max revert instead of truncating.

The exposure review `docs/reviews/EXPOSURE-146-nofloor.md` (2026-10-16, decision 146) runs the launch bid rule with no minute floor (`dropFloorBps` 1) and the spend cap off (`spendCapBps` 10,000) against a 500 credit dump: the compounding drop bounds the dump to 0.86 ETH on a 10 or a 100 ETH pot, the worst minute is 0.44 ETH, no seller is refused while the pot holds ETH, the launch wave clears in one block, recovery is 105 minutes, and steady throughput is 23 credits an hour. Lower `spendCapBps` during an incident.

the pile: one insertion ordered doubly linked list of credit ids, oldest first, with cost basis and acquisition time per credit. every credit enters the pile through `sellForEth` (cost the per-credit bid paid plus its artist royalty), `buyListing` (cost the quote plus its royalty plus the keeper payment), `sellForBalance` (cost `floorRate * score / RATE_SCORE`, the balance royalty is paid in balance token and not recorded) or `adopt` (cost `ethPrice * score / RATE_SCORE`). the royalty is `buyToArtistBps` of the amount paid, on top, decision 163.

## 5. fee intake

the Core is the pool's bounty recipient. `receive()` accepts eth from the hook and the fee escrow only and does nothing else; a plain send from anyone else reverts `UseFund`. every door starts with `_book()`: the eth above `ethPot + ethToBuyback + ethRedeemBurn`, with the door's own `msg.value` excluded, is fee income. the rate is checkpointed first; `payeePpm` of the income is pushed to the payee once `payeeOn`; `feeToRedeemBurnBps` of the rest goes to the redeem burn pot; the remainder goes to the pot. `payeeOn` latches at the first book with income at or after the launch time recorded by the factory plus the current `settings.sniperSeconds` (1,800 at launch), and that book shares nothing, so the anti sniper window's income is never shared (decision 26). the owner or a keeper calls `book()` when the window ends so the latch does not wait for the next door. The latch is one way. This Core setting does not change the pool MEV schedule fixed at launch by the separate config `sniperSeconds`.

other eth enters through doors that book it to the pot whole: `fund()` (payable, anyone, also the predecessor engine on `migrate`) and `collectEscrow()` (claims the Core's escrow credit and books the claim whole to the pot, the refund of a partial buyback fill). `collectEscrow` books the claim whole to the pot. the Core's `receive` is two immutable compares and accepts the hook's 2,300 gas push, so the fee escrow holds buyback refunds in practice; a fee push that ever failed would be booked to the pot without the payee and redeem burn pot shares, an accepted property (decision 125). the payee share is pushed by the book with `SafeTransferLib.forceSafeTransferETH(payee, amount, 50,000)`, so a payee that reverts or runs out of gas never blocks the book. the balance token is held by the module, not the Core, so there is no balance booking door. a stray external `escrow.claim(core, 0)` lands as fee income at the next book.

per 100 eth of volume at launch values: 1.0 eth to the protocol, 9.0 eth to the Core, of which 0 to the payee (payeePpm 0 at launch), 0.9 eth to the reserve, 8.1 eth to the pot.

## 7. compose

`compose()` composes the controller's page, the 80 oldest pile credits, with a format `keccak256(prevrandao, first id) % 8`; `compose(format)` with the given format, 0 to 7. `composeChosen(ids, format)` composes 80 pile credits the caller names: requires `chosenComposeOn`, a CC balance of at least `minCcToChoose`, and the newest named credit past its window. all three run one path: every credit is pulled from the pile (a missing or repeated id reverts), Statements mints to the Core, the record stores the cost (the sum of the credits' costs), the compose time, the rating (the sum of the credits' scores read from Statements) and the pause mark; `composerOf(sid)` stores the caller; `Composed(sid, ids, format, cost, price, caller)`, where `price` is the controller's `priceOf(sid)` read after the record is written with at most 400,000 gas, 0 when the read reverts, runs out of gas or answers short. the caller is paid `min(counted gas, 13,700,000) * min(basefee, callerMaxBaseFee) + callerFee` from the pot, 3 gwei and 0.0003 eth at launch (decision 138). while the credit sale is on, the page is ready only once its newest credit is past `window`.

`setHeldFormat(sid, format)`: anyone with at least `minCcToChoose` CC sets the format of a held statement through `Statements.setFormat`; works while paused and after `migrate`. the balance gate is read at the call. a buyer sets its own format after the sale.

## 8. the sale

the Core sells through two doors the controller alone calls. `sellTo(sid, buyer)` requires a held statement and `msg.value >= cost * saleFloorBps / 10,000`, sends the statement, takes `saleToArtistBps` and `saleToOperatorBps` off the payment and pushes them to the artist and the payee, books `saleToBuybackBps` of the rest to the buyback and the remainder to the pot. `sellCreditTo(id, buyer)` requires a pile credit and `msg.value >= cost * saleFloorBps / 10,000` (decision 154), stores the sale time for the return lock, sends the credit, takes the two cuts off the payment, books `creditCostToPotBps` of the cost (bounded by the rest) to the pot and the remainder of the rest to the buyback. both push the cuts with `forceSafeTransferETH` at 50,000 gas and both revert `Paused` while paused.

the controller prices. `pointRate = floorRate / RATE_SCORE` (wei per score unit; `RATE_SCORE` is 100 points in score units). `valueOf(points) = max(floorRate * points / RATE_SCORE, pool value of points * unit)`, where the pool term prices `points * unit` balance token base units at the balance pool's sqrt price and is 0 when the pool key or the module is unset, the unit read fails or exceeds `uint128.max`, the pool reads zero or a product exceeds 256 bits. the reference of a statement is `max(cost, valueOf(rating))`. the asking price is the reference times a bps curve: start `min(anchorBps * markupBps / 10,000, startCapBps)`, falling in a straight line to `saleFloorBps` of the reference over `decayHours` hours and resting there, computed at each call from the statement's age (`statementAge`, which excludes paused seconds) and the current settings. `atFloor(sid)` is gated by the controller's `ageEligibilityOn` (decision 155), false at launch. off, the launch rule compares the curve price with its floor and reads the reference. on, the rule is age only: true once `startBps() <= 10,000` or `statementAge(sid) >= decayHours * 3600`, reading no price or pool, so eligibility never restarts on a price move and never waits on a quote. at launch `saleFloorBps` is 10,000, so the two rules share the same age boundary. the anchor is 10,000 plus an excess that halves every `anchorHalfLife`; a `buy` of a statement costing at least `anchorMinCost` at a price above the anchor (in bps of the reference) moves it by `floor((saleBps - currentAnchor) * anchorMoveBps / 10,000)`, at most `startCapBps`. The launch fraction is 0 (decision 154): no sale moves the anchor and its clock stays untouched, so the start is `markupBps` of the reference, 150 percent, falling to the floor over `decayHours` 4 hours; 10,000 would move it fully to the sale.

in value mode (section 4) `valueOf(points)` is `points * unit` balance token base units at `P_sell`, rounded up, with the floor rate term dropped, and reverts `NoQuote` while the quote is unusable; a statement's reference is `valueOf(rating) * floorMultBps() / 10,000`, the cost term dropped, and the curve rests at 10,000 of it instead of `saleFloorBps`; a credit's floor is `valueOf(score) * floorMultBps() / 10,000`, still at least `saleFloorBps` of the cost. `buy`, `buyCredit` and `buyCredits` call `refreshReference()` before they price.

`buy(sid)` takes `price`, forwards it to `sellTo`, refunds the excess last. the artist and operator cuts come off the payment inside `sellTo`.

the credit sale window (`saleOn`): every pile credit is for sale from its acquisition: `creditPrice(id)` falls in a straight line from `floor * startBps / 10,000` to `floor = max(cost, valueOf(score)) * floorMultBps() / 10,000` over `window` and rests there, where `floorMultBps() = max(10,000, (10,000 + creditSpreadBps) * balanceBidCapBps / 10,000)` reads the Core's cap live (11,000 at launch with the spread 1,000 of decision 155, 12,100 at the hard cap of 11,000; decisions 150, 152, 153, 155), and at least `saleFloorBps` of the cost, so the quote is always a price `sellCreditTo` takes. `valueOf(score)` is at least `floorRate * score / RATE_SCORE`, which is at least the per-credit eth bid `ethRate() * score / RATE_SCORE`, so a buy followed by `sellForEth` in the next second cannot profit, and the return lock refuses the credit for six hours at launch, time for the pool to move back after a manipulation. `buyCredit(id)` and `buyCredits(ids)` take the price each and forward it to `sellCreditTo`, which takes the two cuts off the top. `pastWindow(acquiredAt)` is true while the sale is off or `window` has passed; compose takes only credits past their window.

the pause: `setPaused(bool)` on the Core, owner only. `pausedTotal` accumulates closed pauses and `pausedSince` marks the open one; a statement's record stores the paused seconds at its compose, so its age `(now - composedAt) - (paused seconds since compose)` holds through a pause. the credit window and the anchor run on wall time.

## 9. the balance side

in value mode (section 4) the eth bid prices off the balance price state instance: an eth fill drops it, its clamp is `balanceBidCapBps`, and a module set leaves it. the balance bid never reads or moves it (decision 164).

the module holds the balance token; the Core holds none (decision 117). the module's interface adds `available()`, the token it commits to the bid (it may hold more and keep the rest for other uses), and `pay(to, amount)`, the Core only, which moves `amount` to `to` and reverts above `available()`. a redemption's proceeds stay in the module. the bid off (`balanceBidOn` false) is the accumulate mode: redemptions keep filling the module while `redeemOn`, and nothing leaves the module through the Core until the owner turns the bid on. the bid opens at the posted price when the owner turns it on. a module that spends its holding elsewhere halts the bid at `PotTooSmall`; the balance side is optional by design.

the balance bid is a flat posted price (decision 164): `balanceRate()` is `10,000 - balanceDiscountBps` bps of par in every mode, bid on or off, and `balanceBidOf(id)` is `score * unit * balanceRate() / 10,000`, 0 when no module is set, the unit read fails, or the payout plus its artist royalty exceeds `available()` (the view never advertises a payout the fill refuses). in value mode each credit's posted payout is lowered to the controller's `balanceClampFor(score, posted)`, the eth bid for the score valued in tokens at the protected sale price per token, so a filled payout is worth at most the eth bid when the engine sells the tokens and a cross-currency round trip cannot extract a spot that leads the reference (decision 165); the royalty and the `available()` bound follow the clamped payout, and `balanceBidOf` and `balanceBidForScore` report it, so the view equals the fill. outside value mode the posted payout is par minus the discount, unchanged. `sellForBalance` pays each credit that payout, unchanged across fills and over time, and pays the artist `buyToArtistBps` of it on top in balance token through a second module `pay` (decision 163); the running total of payouts and royalties must not pass `available()` (`PotTooSmall`). the route's payoff is known in the unit it is paid in (100 points redeem for 100 balance token), so the price is par less a margin, and the discount is also the buffer that keeps the engine's bid from being the binding arbitrage between the credit market and the balance token price (composition arbitrage pays whenever credits trade under par, the engine's bid only once they trade more than the discount under). the credit enters the eth pile at cost basis `floorRate * score / RATE_SCORE`, at least 1 wei. a credit the engine sold through `sellCreditTo` within `returnLockSeconds` is refused (`ReturnLocked`, section 6). `BalanceFill` logs each credit's payout, its royalty, and the available left after both. the balance price state instance (`State.balance`) remains as value mode's eth instance only: `balanceBidMarginBps` and `balanceBidCapBps` are its floor and cap, the value switch resets it, and a module set never touches it.

`redeemStatement(sid)`: requires a module and `redeemOn`, a held statement, and `atFloor(sid)` from the controller (a bounded read of 400,000 gas; a failure reads as not eligible). the Core forgets the statement, hands it to the module, calls `redeem(sid)`, and requires the module's balance token holding to rise by at least `rating * unit * (10,000 - redeemSlippageBps) / 10,000`, with `unit` the module's `unitPerPoint()` read in the call. a failed, zero or oversized unit read, or a holding that rises by less than that, reverts `BadModule`. the proceeds stay in the module. `redeemBurnBps` of the redeem burn pot moves to the buyback. the caller is paid like a composer.

`setBalanceModule(module)`, owner only, any number of times until `lockBalanceModule`: the module must have code, report a balanceToken with code, which may change at an explicit owner module set (decision 142), pass the forbidden list, report a valid unit at the set, and answer `available()`. The module's current holdings stay in that module when it is replaced; a currency change leaves those tokens in their module as stored. Re-select the previous module to resume using its reserve. A pool key prices value while its currency matches the selected token. Set the replacement's complete pool key before resuming sales that rely on its value floor. Leave the module door unlocked while replacements are wanted, and handle any old module reserves before locking the door. `lockBalanceModule` reverts while no module is set. `releaseRedeemBurn(amount)` moves reserve eth to the buyback at any time.

## 10. buyback

`buyback()`, anyone, at most once per `buybackDelay` blocks: takes `min(buybackSlice, ethToBuyback)`, swaps exact eth in for coin on the canonical pool through the pool manager's unlock, burns the coin on the token, and pays the caller `min(counted gas, 600,000) * min(basefee, keeperMaxBaseFee) + keeperFee * spent / largest budget`. a slice under twice the largest payment reverts. the hook's skim on this swap reaches the Core during the swap and is fee income at the next book. there is no minimum output; the slice bound and the pool depth limit a sandwich (decision 51; lower the slice after a large price fall, RUNBOOK section 4).

| as coin admin: `setBountyRecipient`, `lockRecipients`, `setAllowed`, `lockAllowlist`, `unrestrict`, `renounceAdmin` | the fee stream migration switch is the bounty recipient; `lockRecipients` seals it and is taken with `lockSuccessor` (decision 109) |

the hard rule: eth and credits leave the Core at the owner's call only through `migrate`, to the successor. the balance token is held by the module, which has no owner. it leaves the module through the Core's `pay` or `redeem` path, or through `migrateReserve`, which anyone may call after the Core retires and which sends the module's whole holding to the successor Core's current module when that module is bound to the successor and to the same token. the Core moves it only at a seller's call, bounded by `score * unit * balanceBidCapBps / 10,000` per credit and by the module's `available()`. every other outflow is capped by a bound: the bid for a credit, `keeperFee` and `keeperMaxBaseFee` times the gas caps for the keeper doors, `callerFee` and `callerMaxBaseFee` times the gas caps for compose and redeem, the floors for the sale doors, and the three forced pushes bounded by their share of the payment or the income (the artist cut `saleToArtistBps`, the operator cut `saleToOperatorBps`, together strictly below 10,000 of 10,000, and the payee fee share `payeePpm`, at most 1,000,000 of 1,000,000), each sent with a 50,000 gas forced transfer so a reverting recipient never blocks a sale or a book. `setPayee` redirects the payee push and the operator cut, `setArtist` redirects the artist cut.

what the rule does not cover, accepted by the owner (decisions 8, 9.4): the owner sets the price the engine pays, so a dishonest owner or a stolen key can sell credits to the engine at an inflated rate and drain the pot, set a module that returns dust for statements or a unit that overpays the balance bid, swap the controller and sell every statement at the floor, and move everything to a successor at once. holders trust the owner key fully. mitigations: a multisig owner, the events on every setter, the locks.

## 12. launch

eleven transactions, all but the owner calls and the launch through the deterministic deployer (CREATE2) at addresses that depend on the config and the salts, never on the deployer's nonce: the library, the Core (its constructor takes the artist and computes the coin address through the factory's `predictToken` with itself as bounty recipient), the controller (built with the Core address), the Seaport adapter, the CreditStrategy adapter, the coin renderer (built with the config owner, so its address does not depend on the Core), `setCore` on the renderer (the one time bind of the Core), `setController`, `addAdapter` twice, and the launch through `deployToken` with the deploy fee (0 at the pin, the factory's `deployFee()`), which names the renderer in the token config. that is eleven transactions. the artist is a Core constructor argument, so there is no separate `setArtist` transaction. historically measured before issue 23 on a fork of block 26186912: 16,120,826 gas in all, the Core creation 5,242,833, the launch 3,627,708; every transaction under the 16,777,216 cap. the factory owner key signs every transaction and is the Core's owner, the config owner and the payee. before the launch the factory owner lowers `minProtocolSkimShareBps` to 1,000. after the launch: `book()` at the end of the anti sniper window. `lockRecipients` is a later owner decision. the runbook is docs/RUNBOOK.md.

## 14. accepted properties and risks

| # | item |
|---|---|
| 1 | the owner changes every economic number at once; the bounds are the only limit; holders trust the owner |
| 2 | `saleFloorBps` can be set to zero; a change reaches every held statement at once |
| 3 | `redeemStatement` is permissionless: with `redeemOn` anyone redeems a statement at its floor at whatever the balance token is worth |
| 4 | the skim rates are fixed in the pool; the bounty recipient is the coin admin's switch until `lockRecipients` |
| 5 | the coin is restricted: trading works only through the canonical pool; the owner can `unrestrict` |
| 6 | the buyback has no minimum output; the slice and the pool depth bound a sandwich |
| 7 | a fee on transfer or rebasing balance token is unsupported |
| 8 | credits sent to the Core outside the doors sit in no pile until `adopt`, and `rescueNft` does not return them; eth from a non hook sender must come through `fund` |
| 9 | the stack and the coin are deploy inputs; a new stack is a new Core; a new Core on the same coin takes the fee stream through the coin admin's `setBountyRecipient` |
| 10 | the module door stays open until `lockBalanceModule`; a dishonest module takes every statement for dust |
| 11 | a pool price pushed down lowers `valueOf` to the rate term and never below cost; pushed up, it raises asks and a buyer pays the quote or waits; the cost basis of a balance bought credit reads no pool |
| 12 | raising the anchor can delay a redemption until the curve falls back |
| 13 | a restyle can front run a purchase; the buyer restyles after the sale. the `minCcToChoose` gate is a balance read at the call |
| 14 | the payee latch: fees that arrive between the end of the sniper window and the first `book()` are not shared; the owner calls `book()` at the window's end |
| 15 | a tip in a Seaport order to the offerer or an allowlisted recipient raises the engine's payment up to the bid, the price `sellForEth` pays |
| 16 | mainnet caps a transaction at 16,777,216 gas; `compose` uses about 8.5 million after refunds and a `sellForEth` batch stops at about 91 credits (per point, each credit reads its score) |
| 17 | the engine launches on AI run reviews (docs/reviews/) with no human audit |
| 18 | `swapCredit` treats a higher score as more desirable: it takes an incoming credit of equal or higher score and holds the pile's total score. a holder who values a lower score credit more cannot swap for it; the `swapFeeCc` burn and the score floor are the only gates |
| 19 | the controller's credit sale floor `max(cost, valueOf(score)) * floorMultBps` reads the balance token pool spot price (`getSlot0`) for the `valueOf` pool term. the return lock refuses the credit at every intake door for `returnLockSeconds` (six hours at launch); after the lock the floor follows the pool spot. a time weighted price for the floor is an open item (STATUS section 3) |
| 20 | `collectEscrow` books the claim whole to the pot. the Core's `receive` is two immutable compares and accepts the hook's 2,300 gas push, so the fee escrow holds buyback refunds in practice; a fee push that ever failed would be booked to the pot without the payee and redeem burn pot shares, an accepted property. |
| 21 | value mode (decision 148): a pool price held away from the reference longer than the rate limit moves the reference the full way; the owner's pause is the backstop and the web shows spot against reference. the quote clamps to the spot and liquidity sampled at the last refresh, a prior block, so a same-block pool move or one-block liquidity cannot change it (decision 159, C01, C04). a stale or thin quote no longer halts the eth bid: it falls back to the last good value rate and a balance bought credit records that rate as its basis (decision 159, C02); the keeper's `refreshReference` tick keeps the reference fresh and is paid from the pending income it books. a hostile controller pays at most `rateCap`, cannot move the balance bid and cannot block conversion |
| 22 | the balance bid is a flat posted price bounded by the module's `available()` alone (decision 164): it has no drop, so a dump drains the reserve at the posted price until it is refilled by redemptions; every such fill is a gain in balance token terms (paid under par, redeemed at par), and the discount is the buffer against the engine being the binding arbitrage when credits trade under par. the artist royalty on purchases (decision 163) is a pot outflow of `buyToArtistBps` of every purchase, about 2.4 percent of the pot's spending at launch |
